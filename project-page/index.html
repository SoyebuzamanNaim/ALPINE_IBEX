THE PROBLEM

NASA has the evidence. Finding the right evidence is the hard part.

Microgravity fundamentally changes combustion: buoyant convection vanishes, flame geometry rounds into spherical caps, oxygen transport relies strictly on cabin ventilation, and extinction boundaries shift dramatically compared to Earth gravity.

NASA has accumulated invaluable evidence across decades of spaceflight investigations. However, these experiments differ in fuel material, ambient oxygen, cabin pressure, forced airflow, sample geometry, and archival format across scattered technical PDFs.

A mission safety engineer evaluating a spacecraft scenario must determine which flight tests are truly physically comparable, whether a quantitative model is scientifically justified, and where every empirical claim originated.

EVIDENCE BEFORE ELOQUENCE

Every result has an epistemic identity.

FLARE-X separates direct NASA spaceflight observations from machine learning inferences and AI-synthesized reasoning. The multi-agent Evidence Auditor validates numerical claims against source records before presenting them.

NASA OBSERVATION Directly recorded spaceflight experiment from BASS, DARTFire, or SAFFIRE flight archives.
MODEL INFERENCE Probabilistic prediction generated by a validated FLARE-X quantitative ML model within verified envelope boundaries.
AI SYNTHESIS Physical reasoning and comparative explanation grounded in retrieved experimental records and domain literature.
LIMITATION Explicit boundary conditions, scale caveats, and warnings defining where extrapolation cannot be scientifically trusted.

SCIENTIFIC BOUNDARIES

Insufficient evidence is a valid result.

FLARE-X is an advanced research and scientific decision-support prototype developed for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026. It is not certified operational flight software or mission-critical safety clearance guidance.

NASA microgravity combustion investigations examine specific materials, sample geometries, and chamber flow conditions. Controlled similarity in laboratory ducts does not guarantee identical behavior in full-scale spacecraft architectures.

NASA Data Sources & Investigation Catalog

Primary NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) Accessions

FLARE-X indexes discrete experimental runs and technical reports across 5 core spaceflight combustion families:

  • BASS-II (PSI-25): Burning and Suppression of Solids II. Tested in the Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG) aboard ISS. Variable oxygen, pressure, and forced duct ventilation.
  • BASS (PSI-26): Initial Burning and Suppression of Solids investigations on PMMA, Delrin, and fabric samples.
  • FLEX / FLEX-2 (PSI-69, PSI-68): Flame Extinction Experiments inside the Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR).
  • SPICE (PSI-107): Smoke Point in Co-flow Experiment. 526 experimental gas diffusion flames.
  • SAFFIRE (PSI-98, PSI-99, PSI-100): Spacecraft Fire Safety experiments conducted on Cygnus resupply vehicles.

Scientific Boundaries & Research Prototype Disclosures

Scientific Grounding & Scope Caveats

FLARE-X is an evidence-bounded research prototype designed for scientific decision-support and historical flight test comparison. It operates under strict epistemic rules:

  • Not Operational Flight Software: FLARE-X is not certified for mission-critical fire safety clearance or spacecraft atmosphere authorization.
  • The Evidence Absence Rule: A lack of recorded spaceflight flammability data is never interpreted as evidence of safety.
  • Section 7 Zero-Extrapolation Guard: Whenever scenario inputs fall outside the verified convex hull of NASA flight tests, prediction is withheld.
