Too little airflow and a flame smothers. Too much and it blows out. In orbit the window between them is narrow, and it is not where tests on Earth say it is.

Open the tool 129 flight tests · NASA PSI · CC0

The problem

Earth is the wrong test.

On the ground, hot gas rises and drags fresh air into a flame. In orbit there is no up, so a fire is fed only by the cabin ventilation around it.

NASA measured the gap. Standard upward-burning limits on Earth are not conservative for microgravity — by up to four percentage points of oxygen. A material cleared on the ground can burn in space.

The data

Twenty years of fires, 129 of them.

You cannot light a fire aboard a crewed spacecraft whenever you like. That is why two decades of research is only 129 test conditions, each one flown.

They come from NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics repository as a published dataset with a DOI, not from a reading of a PDF. Every value here is the one NASA recorded.

The tool

A distance, not a verdict.

Set an atmosphere and an airflow. The tool reports how far that condition sits from NASA's own published limit, in percentage points of oxygen.

It reports a margin rather than a yes or no, because a margin is what an operator can act on and a label is not.

Where it stops

It says when it does not know.

Ask about a material NASA never flew, or a condition outside the tested envelope, and the answer is that there is no answer — not a confident guess.

Of the 28 recorded non-propagating results, 17 are flames that never took hold at all, 8 were smothered and 3 were blown out. Those are three different questions, and the tool keeps them apart.
